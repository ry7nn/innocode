04 — Why now

The cheapest tonne of carbon is the one you already paid for.

i.

Energy stopped being cheap

Industrial fuel and power are no longer a rounding error on a plant's P&L. Every wasted megawatt-hour now has a visible owner inside the business — which is exactly when behaviour changes.

ii.

Diversion is a cost centre

Landfill gate fees, haulage and effluent charges are pure loss. Symbiosis turns a disposal budget into a supply contract without a single new machine on site.

iii.

Industrial estates are the unit

Loops work at the scale of a zone, not a company. Put the map in one place — heat, water, CO₂, solids — and the cheapest projects almost always cross a property boundary.

05 — Assumptions

Every number on this page, in one place.

These constants are illustrative and deliberately conservative. They are grouped here so they're easy to swap for metered or sourced figures — and easy to defend.

ConstantValueWhat it means
ENERGY_PRICE250 AED / MWh Marginal cost of the industrial heat avoided — not the full retail tariff. Replace with the plant's actual fuel or steam cost.
RECOVERY60% Share of an identified waste stream that a well-designed recovery loop actually reuses. The rest is losses, turndown and mismatched timing.
CO2_PER_MWH0.4 t Grid/fuel emission factor applied to each avoided megawatt-hour. Swap in the local factor for the site.
DIVERSION50% Share of the disposal cost that disappears when a stream finds a buyer. Half is a deliberately cautious default.

Before a demo: replace all four with the figures from one real site, and say where each came from. A judge will ask, and "it's in the assumptions table" is a good answer.
